About the project

California Drought Impact is an interactive multimodal data artwork about the drought, built from laser-cut wooden data sculptures, projection mapping and a digital visualization. The digital part went through California’s map data, drew lines as streams and let people explore the reservoirs. It was built in 2016 with D3.js, JSON, Python and jQuery. This page rebuilds it with D3 v7 and three.js.

Concept and visual design: Yoon Chung Han (principal investigator). Development: Shankar Tiwari, student research assistant, California State University, Fullerton. Original project page · 2016 version

Reservoir storage (sensor 15, acre-feet) and river flow (sensor 41, monthly mean of daily cfs) come from the California Data Exchange Center, to . Each monthly value is end-of-month storage; recent months are provisional until the operators revise them, and short gaps are filled and flagged. Only of the river gauges report daily flow, so the other links are drawn at a fixed width. Locations come from the original 2016 dataset; capacities do too, except where CDEC has since revised them. Snowpack is the California Cooperative Snow Surveys' snow-pillow network on CDEC (sensor 3, snow water equivalent): the reading on the 1st of each month at the stations CDEC lists for its North, Central and South Sierra sections, summed and divided by the sum of those stations' April 1 averages. A month with too few stations reporting shows "No data". The 3D snowline follows each section's figure; it is drawn, not mapped.
